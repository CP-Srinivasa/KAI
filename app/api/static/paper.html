How KAI Falsifies and Seals Market Claims

Anyone can publish a track record. KAI fixes every claim before the data exists, measures it by machine under a declared cost model, and seals the verdict — whatever it was — where no one can edit it afterwards.

Abstract

Anyone can claim a hit rate; post-hoc selection and survivorship make most public signal claims unverifiable. KAI is a solo-operated research platform built the other way around: every market-signal hypothesis is registered deterministically before measurement (the claim's identity is a hash over the normalized hypothesis including its machine-readable pass thresholds), measured fill-independently under an explicit cost model, judged mechanically (fail-closed — no human transcribes numbers into the verdict), and the verdict sealed in a hash-chained ledger anchored into Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps. A codified stop rule declares a hypothesis family terminally dead after three pre-registered failures — which actually happened to the news-direction family. The most credible evidence is self-application: KAI falsified and sealed its own alpha claim (n=68 closed paper trades, P(mu_net>0)=10.44%, median −93.8 bps net; removing the single best trade drops P to 3.50%). An attested report was reproduced byte-exactly on a second machine — across OS, architecture and Python-version boundaries. The limits are stated plainly: sealing proves integrity and time of existence, not correctness; external demand is unproven, not disproven.

01

The problem: track records are cheap

Anyone can publish a track record. The mechanics of doing so dishonestly are well understood and mostly do not even require lying:

  • Post-hoc selection. Run a hundred variations of a strategy, publish the one that worked, quietly forget the rest. A useless strategy evaluated across roughly a hundred untracked trials produces an expected maximum Sharpe ratio of about 2.5 from selection luck alone. Every untracked trial silently raises the bar an honest result would have to clear.
  • Moving goalposts. Define "success" after seeing the data. A 1-hour horizon becomes "well, it works at 4 hours"; a net loss becomes "gross of costs it was positive."
  • Survivorship. Dead strategies, dead accounts and dead funds disappear from the sample; the survivors look like skill.
  • Unverifiable claims. Even an honest researcher usually publishes numbers no third party can recompute: the input data, the exact code state, and the evaluation window are gone or were never pinned.

The result is a market for signal claims in which the honest and the dishonest are observationally almost identical. KAI's methodology is built to make one specific kind of statement possible: this claim was fixed before the data existed, measured by a machine under a declared cost model, and the verdict — whatever it was — cannot have been altered afterwards, and can be recomputed by someone who does not trust us.

KAI is a solo-operated research platform. It emerged from a trading-bot project that spent months building measurement infrastructure — and then used that infrastructure to falsify its own premise (§5). What survived is the measurement machinery itself.

02

The discipline: pre-register, measure, judge mechanically, stop

Four mechanisms, all running in production today, none of them aspirational.

2.1 // Pre-registration before measurement

A hypothesis enters the system through a CLI command (trading prereg-register) before any evaluation runs. A registration fixes the claim (name, direction, horizon), the success criteria as free text and as a machine-readable gate — a JSON object of pass thresholds — and a sample-size target.

The registration's identity is deterministic: a SHA-256 over the whitespace- and case-normalized claim, truncated to a 16-hex key. The gate JSON is part of the hashed identity — changing any threshold changes the ID, so a silently relaxed pass bar is a different, visibly new claim, not an edit. A real entry from the ledger:

prereg_id: f676bcf5a7a1bfb6
name:      funding_premium_meanrev_1h
schema:    prereg/v1

Eight hypotheses are currently registered. Pre-registration is record-only: it gates no trade and touches no execution path. Its entire job is to make later claims auditable against what was committed in advance.

2.2 // Fill-independent measurement, explicit costs

Outcomes are not measured from the platform's own order fills. Fills entangle a hypothesis with execution luck, queue position and portfolio state. Instead, hypotheses are evaluated against a shadow-outcome pool — currently roughly 8,000 resolved forward outcomes computed from recorded market data, independent of whether any order ever existed. Costs are not an afterthought: the evaluation applies a versioned, explicit cost model (currently 20 bps round-trip, cost model version 1.1.0), and gross figures are computed alongside net. A claim that only survives gross of costs fails.

2.3 // Mechanical verdicts

The verdict step (trading prereg-check) compares measured metrics against the registered gate JSON. It is fail-closed: missing data, ambiguous fields or an unresolvable gate produce a non-pass, never a benefit of the doubt. No human transcribes numbers into the verdict, which removes the most common corruption point in research reporting — the step where "0.104" becomes "roughly significant" in prose.

2.4 // Family stop rules

Falsification without a stop rule degenerates into whack-a-mole: the backlog refills with variations of already-dead ideas. KAI codifies the stop rule in code (changing it requires a reviewed pull request):

STOP_RULE_FAILS = 3
# 3 pre-registered falsifications across DISTINCT constructions
# => the hypothesis family is TERMINAL_DEAD.

Registering a new hypothesis into a terminal-dead family is refused unless explicitly overridden, and the override itself is recorded. This is not hypothetical: the news_direction family — "news sentiment predicts short-horizon price direction" — was declared terminal dead after three pre-registered failed constructions. That family can no longer quietly consume research budget.

03

The sealing: what each layer proves — and what it does not

A verdict that can be edited after the fact is worthless. KAI seals its outputs in three layers.

  • Layer 1 — canonical content hash. Every attested payload is rendered as canonical JSON — keys sorted at every depth, compact separators, UTF-8, NaN/Infinity rejected — and hashed with SHA-256. The same logical content always yields the same bytes and the same hash, regardless of machine or key insertion order. This proves integrity of one claim.
  • Layer 2 — hash-chained ledger. Each attestation record binds its payload hash, a sequence number, and the hash of the previous record into an append-only JSONL ledger (genesis is 64 zero bytes). Removing, reordering or editing any historical record breaks the chain visibly for anyone holding a copy. The writer is fail-closed: if the ledger tail is unreadable, the system refuses to extend the chain rather than silently fork history. This proves integrity of the history — not just "this verdict is untampered" but "no verdict has been deleted."
  • Layer 3 — Bitcoin anchoring via OpenTimestamps. Ledger and audit digests are stamped through OpenTimestamps against redundant public calendar servers, committing them into the Bitcoin blockchain. This proves existence at a time against an external, permissionless system: the operator cannot backdate a claim even if he controls every machine involved.

What none of this proves: correctness. A sealed report with a wrong number in it is still wrong — merely immutably and timestampably wrong. Sealing converts "trust me, this is what I said" into "verify that this is what I said, and when." Whether what was said is true requires the fourth property: independent recomputation.

04

The reproduction: the missing keystone, and how it was closed

Until this month, no sealed KAI report had ever been recomputed by anyone outside the production machine. That was the honest gap in the whole construction, so it was tested deliberately.

The experiment. Take the most recent attested canonical-edge report (ledger seq 11, payload hash a524d6c1…c33c91), produced on the production host — Linux/ARM, Python 3.12 — and attempt full recomputation on an independent machine: a Windows 11 workstation, x64, Python 3.13, from a fresh checkout of the same code commit and read-only copies of the two input artifacts.

  • Result A — the ledger layer verified fully offline. All 21 ledger records checked out on the second machine: per-record payload hash, record-hash integrity, chain linkage, no sequence gaps. This layer already met the "any third party" bar.
  • Result B — a naive re-run diverged, and the divergence was instructive. Running the same CLI on the copied inputs produced different numbers (75 trades became 76). Cause: the inputs are append-only audit logs that had grown for ~seven hours between attestation and copy, and nothing pinned which file state the computation consumed. Without input pinning, "reproduce the report" is not even a well-defined instruction.
  • Result C — with the window fixed, the reproduction was byte-exact. Bounding the evaluation window to the attested end timestamp reproduced the entire payload bit-identically — the recomputed SHA-256 equals the attested payload hash — across an OS boundary, an architecture boundary and a Python version boundary. The determinism is by construction: canonical JSON, a seeded bootstrap for all probabilistic figures, pure file inputs, no dependence on clock, environment or database.

The gaps found were closed the same day, in code: every attested report now seals the exact input state it consumed ({role, path, sha256, lines}); the evaluation window is a CLI flag (--until); one-command third-party verification exists (trading canonical-edge --verify <seq> recomputes the sealed claim from pinned inputs and exits 0 or 1); and the code commit is co-attested so drift is visible.

Honest remaining limits. The input artifacts (~38 MB of audit JSONL) currently live only on the production node. A third party without access can verify layers 1–3 — content integrity, chain integrity, Bitcoin timestamp — but cannot yet recompute the numbers. For that hash layer a dependency-free reproduction bundle now exists: a copy of the attestation ledger plus a small standalone verifier (Python standard library only, no KAI code) that recomputes every payload hash, every record hash and the full chain linkage — demonstrated on an unrelated machine against the live ledger (VERIFY OK, exit 0). The full numeric reproduction package is still gated, because those inputs live only on the production node — two different trust levels, and we say so. Reproduction verifies computation, not data collection: a byte-exact reproduction faithfully repeats a wrong upstream price.

05

The credibility asset: we falsified our own alpha claim

Most entities that evaluate trading signals have a structural conflict of interest: they sell what survives. KAI's first fully sealed result points the machinery at its own premise. The platform began as an alpha-seeking trading bot. Its accessible signal families were tested with the discipline above — and fell, one after another.

The negative verdicts are produced and sealed by exactly the machinery being offered — a live demonstration, at the operator's own expense. The platform now sells no signals; its only output is verdicts. An auditor with no signal to sell has no incentive to find survivors.

Scope honesty: these results falsify the tested, retail-accessible signal families under a declared cost model. They are not a claim that no market edge exists anywhere for anyone.

06

What this enables

Three concrete applications, in increasing order of ambition — with their current status stated plainly.

  1. Independent signal audits. A third party's track record can be run through the same pipeline: pre-registered criteria, fill-independent outcomes, explicit costs, mechanical verdict, sealed result. A first pilot audit of an anonymized external channel has been run (below); its honest verdict was "not provable, n too small". Status: pilot, not a product.
  2. A pre-registration notary for research calls. A researcher who wants a public call believed later can register its deterministic claim ID into the ledger before the outcome is known; the hash chain plus the Bitcoin anchor prove precedence without trusting the operator's clock or database. Status: primitives exist and run daily; a public interface does not.
  3. Sealed truth feeds. Deterministic data series (the existing example is Bitcoin fee/mempool statistics, served over an L402 paid endpoint) published together with their attestation, so a consumer can verify integrity and freshness instead of assuming it. Status: the endpoint exists; demand for it is unproven, not disproven — it was gated off and never publicly listed until recently.

The platform's methodology forbids it from overclaiming its own prospects: it is solo-operated; external demand is untested; and the next milestone is not a feature but an external validation event — one person outside the project reproducing one sealed report and saying so. The verification path for that person is now one command.

Worked example :: pilot audit

Signal audit — pilot: an anonymized third-party channel

An honest signal audit, not performance marketing. Its result is a non-result — and that is the point: the same measurement and falsification discipline the platform used to refute its own alpha claim, applied to someone else's channel. The kill criterion was sealed before publication.

What was audited, data basis, period

Audited was the realized net performance of the signals of "Channel A" — an anonymized third-party signal channel (crypto, long-only, microcap-heavy) — exactly as a paper measurement instrument executed them under an explicit cost model: no backtest, no after-the-fact price reconstruction, but the timestamp-exact live record of an automated paper-execution pipeline (ingestion → envelope → approval → bridge → paper engine), every step append-only audited. Period: 2026-04-29 to 2026-07-02 (65 days). 65 signals (~1/day), all long, 50 distinct symbols (USDT pairs, mostly microcaps).

"Not provable (n too small)" — on the measurement-valid data basis (11 resolved signals, June 2026), neither a positive edge nor its absence can be established. The point estimate is slightly positive (+49 bps net per signal), the 95% band [−185; +341] includes zero broadly, and the sign already flips on removing a single trade (−75 bps). A robust verdict requires on the order of n ≥ 100–200 resolved signals under a constant measurement regime.

The value of the process itself: the audit identified and excluded 4 administrative closes, 2 degenerate underflow positions, and 1 price-feed artifact (a +7,398 bps trade containing a +140% intraday jump from a documented feed-corruption era) — exactly the silent contaminations that inflate a naive "hit rate" report. Each exclusion lowers the reported channel performance, i.e. it is conservative against the channel.

Stated limitations: 62% of signals were never opened (selection gap of unknown direction) · paper ≠ live (slippage/fee model, microcap liquidity) · exactly one pre-chosen measure, no horizon shopping · no benchmark baseline at this n · a single channel says nothing about the population of similar channels.

Verifiability & the pre-sealed criterion

The data basis is sha256-sealed (append-only snapshot, 2026-07-02); the per-signal result table is deliberately not published — symbol-date pairs would re-identify the channel — but is contained in the sealed k1_final.json and can be verified on reasoned request.
